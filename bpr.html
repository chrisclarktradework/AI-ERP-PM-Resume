BPR / BPM Experience

How I document, redesign and govern business processes. One manufacturing case, end to end: evidence classified, current state mapped, gaps registered, rules decided with owners, future state mapped with controls and the documentation handed over as a versioned process library.

Midwest Engine Works is a made up company. The numbers are illustrative. The method is the one I use on real engagements.

The case

Engine builder and overhauler. Two plants, one repair depot, three legal entities. Live on Oracle Fusion Cloud Financials, Procurement, Inventory, Cost Management, Manufacturing and Order Management. The processes were never documented. The business runs steps outside the system and the ticket backlog grows. The job is to map what the business does, redesign it and govern it.

Working principle. A ticket is a symptom. The map finds the step where the business and the system disagree. The fix is a decision, a configuration change and a control. The decision has an owner and the control appears on the future state map.

Before and after

The whole redesign on one screen. The left column is what the evidence showed. The right column is what the six decisions produce. The two swimlanes below are the same process, procure to pay, before and after.

Before · current state

  • Requests travel by email. The purchase order is keyed after the goods arrive.
  • Receipts are logged on a clipboard when no purchase order exists.
  • Match tolerances sit at zero. Every variance creates a hold with no owner.
  • Holds are chased by email between AP, the buyer and the plant.
  • Accounting and costing run once a month. Errors surface on the last day.
  • Intercompany runs on a spreadsheet. No process map exists.

After · future state

  • Every purchase starts as a requisition in Oracle with approval by amount and cost center.
  • No purchase order, no receipt. Every receipt is against a purchase order line.
  • Tolerances set by the business: 2 percent price, 5 percent quantity.
  • Holds route by rule to a named owner with a three day clock.
  • Accounting and costing run nightly. Exceptions are cleared daily.
  • Intercompany runs as transfer orders. Every process has a versioned map and an owner.

Step 1 · Collect and classify the evidence

Every ticket was tagged on five dimensions: process, root cause class, business severity, Oracle object and map step. Most of the backlog is a few root causes reported many times. The classes separate people and process gaps from system defects, so each gap gets the right fix type.

Figure 3. Tickets by root cause class. More than half are training and process gaps.

The same method applied to the second family. Open work orders, missing resource rates and month end only accounting runs were the gaps. The redesign moves that work to nightly jobs, daily exception review and a calendar with one owner per line.

Governance and handover. Maps, transcripts, decisions and defects go into a searchable process library from week one. Every map is versioned with a named owner. A change to a process starts as a gap, becomes a decision and ends as a new map version and a measured result on the PPM dashboard. The integrator and the support provider inherit the library. The client owns it.

Documentation standard

Four levels. One notation per page. BPMN 2.0 for the swimlanes. The same standard on every engagement, so a new reader knows where to look.

LEVEL 1

Value chain

Source, make, deliver, service, finance. One page for the CFO and the plant manager.

LEVEL 2

Process family

Procure to pay, order to cash, record to report, plan to produce, service and depot repair. Oracle modules named on each box.

LEVEL 3

Swimlane

BPMN 2.0 lanes by role plus an Oracle lane. Every decision, exception path, hand off and control drawn. Ticket counts on steps.

LEVEL 4

Work instruction

Screen by screen with the Oracle task name and the data required. The training and the support provider run on these.

Evidence for the current state

  1. The tickets, classified on five dimensions.
  2. Oracle itself: period close exception reports, audit history, setup pages, workflow rules.
  3. Interviews at the desk, watching the work happen.
  4. The spreadsheets beside Oracle. Every spreadsheet is a step the system does not do.
  5. The outgoing integrator's design documents, treated as intent, not fact.

Tools

Lucidchart for BPMN swimlanes with the Lucid MCP, so Claude drafts a map from an interview transcript and a ticket list. A private searchable process library over the engagement files. Oracle exception reports for the truth. Excel for the ticket analysis. A PPM dashboard that tracks each redesign from gap to decision, owner and measured result.
