The mandate

ACR is early in an AI and automation journey driven from the technology and board level. Two automations are already in flight, the build-versus-buy question is still open. No governance model sits underneath either of them yet.

That combination is the specific moment where AI programs either compound or stall. Automations shipped without a governance charter become things nobody will approve at scale. A platform chosen before the processes are understood becomes a three-year run cost defending a decision made too early.

The proposal is a fixed ninety days rather than another contractor on an open-ended rate. One person carrying both halves: the executive facilitation to align leadership on outcomes, plus the technical depth to make the platform call, design the automations and stand up governance that survives the next acquisition.

What the seat calls for

  • A ranked AI roadmap tied to named KPIs, with owners and sequence
  • A build-versus-buy recommendation defensible to the board
  • A governance charter aligned to NIST AI RMF, sized for ACR today
  • One automation running in production, measured before and after
  • The operating rhythm handed to the PMO and the IT team
  • No dependency left behind. The engagement is designed to end

Why this role exists now

Context from the spring. Chris interviewed with Pat Damon's team for the AI and automation program manager role, including a four-person leadership panel covering enterprise architecture, application support, business systems delivery, plus infrastructure and security. ACR appointed another candidate, then reopened the need in May.

A six-week AI Foundation Sprint was proposed to Thai Vong on 3 July 2026. This page restates that proposal as a full ninety days, carrying it through to a production automation and a clean handover.

ACR is also recruiting a Vice President of Enterprise Applications and Engineering Platforms. A fixed engagement is complementary to that search rather than competing with it. The roadmap, the platform decision and the governance charter are exactly what a new VP would otherwise spend their first two quarters building from scratch.
